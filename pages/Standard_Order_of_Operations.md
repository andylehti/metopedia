<p>{{About|the conventional classroom rule set. For the proposed alternative, see <a href="/Canonical_Order_of_Operations/">Canonical Order of Operations</a>.}}</p>
<p>The <b>Standard Order of Operations</b> (<b>SOO</b>) is the conventional rule set used to evaluate arithmetic expressions written in linear notation. It is commonly taught through mnemonics such as PEMDAS, BEDMAS, BIDMAS, or BODMAS.</p>
<p>Within Metopedia's Canonical Order project, SOO is treated as a legacy convention: useful, widely adopted, and often efficient, but sometimes insufficiently explicit when signs, powers, roots, and grouping interact.</p>
<h2 id="common-sequence">Common sequence</h2>
<p>A common classroom version of the Standard Order is:</p>
<ol>
<li>parentheses or brackets</li>
<li>exponents and roots</li>
<li>multiplication and division</li>
<li>addition and subtraction</li>
</ol>
<p>Multiplication and division are normally evaluated left to right, as are addition and subtraction.</p>
<h2 id="strengths">Strengths</h2>
<p>The Standard Order gives a compact convention for ordinary expressions. It prevents expressions such as <math>2+3\times4&lt;/math> from requiring parentheses around every multiplication. It is also deeply integrated into education, calculators, programming languages, and mathematical typesetting.</p>
<h2 id="canonical-critique">Canonical critique</h2>
<p>The Canonical Order critique is not that SOO fails in every case. It is that SOO often depends on convention where a stricter grammar would require explicit structure. The disputed areas include unary negatives, the base of an exponent, radical notation, and fractional exponents involving signed quantities.</p>
<div class="wikitable-wrap"><table class="wikitable"><tr><th>Issue</th><th>SOO handling</th><th>COO critique</th></tr><tr><td>Unary negative</td><td>Often resolved by precedence convention.</td><td>The sign should be explicitly separated from or included in the base.</td></tr><tr><td>Roots</td><td>Treated by radical notation and exponent notation.</td><td>Roots should be written as fractional exponents.</td></tr><tr><td>Parentheses</td><td>Treated as first operation.</td><td>Parentheses also define the object acted on by later operations.</td></tr><tr><td>Ambiguous input</td><td>Often settled by convention or calculator syntax.</td><td>Ambiguous expressions should be rewritten.</td></tr></table></div>
<h2 id="compatibility">Compatibility</h2>
<p>COO usually agrees with SOO for ordinary arithmetic. The proposed change appears mainly in how expressions are written, taught, and disambiguated. For this reason, COO can be described as a notation and interpretation reform rather than a rejection of arithmetic itself.</p>
