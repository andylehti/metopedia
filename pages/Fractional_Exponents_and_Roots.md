<p><b>Fractional exponents and roots</b> are treated in the Canonical Order of Operations as one operation family. A fractional exponent contains both a power and a root: the numerator gives the power, and the denominator gives the root.</p>
<h2 id="basic-rule">Basic rule</h2>
<p><math display="block"> x^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{x^m} &lt;/math></p>
<p>In canonical notation, the radical form is rewritten as an index form.</p>
<p><math display="block"> \sqrt[n]{x^m}\;\longrightarrow\;x^{m/n} &lt;/math></p>

<h2 id="combined-power-and-root">Combined power and root</h2>
<p>In <math>x^{3/5}&lt;/math>, the numerator &lt;math>3&lt;/math> is the power and the denominator &lt;math>5&lt;/math> is the root. The canonical form keeps both instructions attached to one base.</p>
<p><math display="block"> x^{3/5}=\sqrt[5]{x^3} &lt;/math></p>
<h2 id="nested-roots">Nested roots</h2>
<p>Nested roots become powers of powers.</p>
<p><math display="block"> \sqrt{\sqrt{x}}=\left(x^{1/2}\right)^{1/2}=x^{1/4} &lt;/math></p>
<p><math display="block"> \sqrt[3]{\sqrt{x}}=\left(x^{1/2}\right)^{1/3}=x^{1/6} &lt;/math></p>
<h2 id="negative-and-reciprocal-roots">Negative and reciprocal roots</h2>
<p>Negative exponents and fractional exponents can be combined.</p>
<p><math display="block"> x^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{x^{1/2}} &lt;/math></p>
<p><math display="block"> x^{-m/n}=\frac{1}{x^{m/n}} &lt;/math></p>
<p>This gives one notation family for powers, roots, reciprocal powers, and reciprocal roots.</p>
